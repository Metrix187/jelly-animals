Inside the experiment

Three tree frogs

Each frog is a volumetric mesh of — particles and — tetrahedra, cut from a signed-distance tree frog: a squat, wide body, a broad head with a wide mouth, a bulge over each eye, big folded hind legs and a short stub of a tail, joined with smooth unions. One is built and copied at three sizes. A snapped hex lattice, five tetrahedra per cell, keeps the feet on the floor; all three are solved together with XPBD on a fixed 60 Hz step split into 10 substeps. Lattice nodes of different frogs push each other apart, so they bump, lean and pile up.

Getting about

A jelly has no muscles, so a frog moves by hopping. Every hop is one push given to all of its particles at once: forward, up (a little more at the front, so the head leads) and a turn toward where it wants to go. A rotation fitted to its particles each step tells it which way it faces and whether it is the right way up. On its side or its back, it throws itself into the air with a roll and sees how it lands. Each picks a spot on the table away from the others, hops there, waits, and picks another.

Leap

Now and then a frog leaps: one big push forward and up, with a full turn in the air, and it lands wherever the jelly lands it. Nothing is re-posed for this; the push is the whole move, and the wobble after landing is the animation. A frog that has just been put down leaps at once, so setting one on the table is also a leap.

Flies

The flies are small rigid balls that fall, bounce and roll, and meet the frogs through the same tetrahedra. A frog with room hops to the nearest one; when its mouth is over it, the fly goes in and rides a path embedded in the jelly back to the stomach, where it stays for the better part of a minute. Up to — fit. In the dark they glow: the light fills the jelly round them and pools on the table. The — trapped bubbles in each frog are kept out of the stomach.

Light through jelly

three.js on WebGL 2, with every pass a hand-written shader: a key-light shadow map and a top-down height map for contact shading; the table, the dark layer of each frog (the belly and legs), the eyes, nostrils, flies and bubbles into a 4× MSAA half-float target; each frog’s back faces into its own channel of a depth target, for thickness; then the front faces refract what lies behind and absorb it with Beer–Lambert colour, so the thin eye domes come out bright and the body deep green. The pale throat and eyelids, the dark blotches on the back and the mouth are drawn from rest-space coordinates, so they ride the jelly. Khronos PBR Neutral tone mapping finishes the frame.
